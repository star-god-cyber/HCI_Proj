🌟 Executive Summary: Self-directed interview preparation often traps candidates in an unproductive feedback vacuum characterized by evaluative anxiety, opaque scoring criteria, and an absence of structured reflection. In this project, we designed, built, and empirically evaluated a zero-cost High-Fidelity AI-Assisted Interview Practice System. The prototype realizes two complementary interaction paradigms: (1) Prototype A (Instructional Learning-Oriented) with transparent rubric evaluation, sentence-level audit trails ("Why This Feedback?"), metacognitive reflection, and attempt-to-attempt comparison diffs; and (2) Prototype B (Conversational Realism-Oriented) with an animated interviewer avatar (Dr. Marcus Vance), hands-free real-time voice interaction (Speech-to-Text & Text-to-Speech), adaptive follow-up probing, and post-interview debriefing. In a formal within-subjects usability evaluation with $N=6$ participants, the system achieved an outstanding mean System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 90.4 / 100 (Grade A+, 96th percentile), with candidates achieving a +4.0 score point improvement in STAR outcome evidence upon guided retry.

The three architectural tiers operate as follows:

2. Conceptual Model & Interaction Metaphors

A. The Metaphor of the "Reflective Mirror" (Prototype A)

Traditional interview coaching platforms act as opaque evaluative judges. Prototype A re-conceptualizes the AI system as an instructional "Reflective Mirror". Instead of delivering an uncalibrated composite grade, the system decomposes the candidate's response across four transparent optical planes: (1) Relevance, (2) Specificity, (3) Evidence & Outcome, and (4) Structure & Clarity. The "Why This Feedback?" audit modal functions as an inspectable magnifying glass, directly linking rubric penalties to exact sentence citations and presenting high-scoring model STAR exemplars. Crucially, the interface enforces a metacognitive reflection step ("What would you change?" and "What did you learn?") before permitting a retry, transforming passive critique into active deliberative learning.

B. The Metaphor of the "Conversational Partner" (Prototype B)

Prototype B embodies the metaphor of an authentic, uninterrupted "Conversational Partner" (Dr. Marcus Vance). In this mode, evaluative feedback is intentionally deferred during active dialogue turns to preserve psychological immersion and professional conversational rhythm. The avatar listens attentively via real-time STT, pauses thoughtfully, and dynamically challenges the candidate with adaptive follow-up inquiries that target unaddressed risks, team conflicts, or missing quantitative metrics. Comprehensive rubric diagnostic debriefing is presented only upon completing the simulated room.

C. Design Space Mapping

The interview interaction design space is mapped across two orthogonal dimensions: Feedback Latency (Immediate Formative vs. Deferred Summative) and Interaction Modality (Textual Reflection vs. Spoken Hands-Free Dialogue). Prototype A occupies the [Immediate Formative × Text/Voice Reflection] quadrant to maximize instructional mastery, while Prototype B occupies the [Deferred Summative × Spoken Real-Time Simulation] quadrant to maximize ecological realism and stress inoculation.

3. Compromises in Prototyping (Horizontal vs. Vertical)

Following established HCI prototyping methodologies (Nielsen, 1993), development under academic project constraints necessitated deliberate trade-offs between horizontal breadth and vertical depth:

Compromise Dimension Design Decision & Implemented Scope Pedagogical & HCI Justification
Horizontal: Role Breadth Focused deeply on three representative roles (UX Research Intern, Associate Product Manager, Software Engineer) rather than a shallow directory of 50+ job titles. Enabled deep curation of authentic behavioral questions and validated STAR rubrics matching verified student survey personas (Aditi & Rahul).
Horizontal: Interview Types Constrained to Behavioral (STAR) and Situational interviewing, intentionally omitting technical live code editors or whiteboard canvases. Behavioral questions represent the universal barrier reported by 82.8% of surveyed students where feedback transparency is most deficient.
Vertical: Voice Signal Processing Implemented browser-native Web Speech API (STT & TTS) with local offline Vosk fallback, rather than paid cloud APIs (e.g., ElevenLabs or cloud Whisper). Guarantees 100% zero-cost operation, near-zero network latency, and complete user privacy with zero cloud biometric audio transmission.
Vertical: Rubric Evaluation Depth Implemented four inspectable dimensions with deterministic regex-heuristic scoring, augmented by optional Gemini Free API probing. Guarantees 100% reproducible, explainable scoring with sentence-level citations, eliminating LLM hallucination and sycophantic scoring drift.
Vertical: Simulation Turn Length Simulation rooms run 3-question adaptive sequences with one deep follow-up per question rather than a fatiguing 60-minute panel. Prevents cognitive exhaustion during user testing while fully capturing realistic conversational turn-taking and probing dynamics.

4. Prototype Description & Core Functionalities

The high-fidelity implementation fully satisfies the low-fidelity wireframe requirements while introducing rich audio-visual feedback, persistent navigation, and robust state management:

  • Screen A1 (Home & Mode Select): Clear visual toggle between Interview Simulation and Feedback-Based Interview Practice. Includes a persistent top-left Home button accessible from all views.
  • Screen A2 (Role Setup): Candidates select target role, interview type, and difficulty level, with optional CV and JD context integration reflecting survey requirements (82.8% role specificity).
  • Screen A3 (Voice & Text Response): Displays interview question with natural TTS audio playback. Includes real-time speech dictation with animated equalizer waves and interim transcript preview.
  • Screen A4 (Inspectable Rubric Feedback): Calculates quantitative scores across 4 dimensions, accompanied by synthesized qualitative strengths, weaknesses, and actionable recommendations.
  • Screen A5 (Why This Feedback? Audit Modal): Displays inspectable sentence-level citations indicating where points were awarded or docked, paired with a concrete high-scoring model STAR answer.
  • Screen A6 (Metacognitive Reflection) & A7 (Targeted Retry): Prompts candidates to reflect on weaknesses ("What would you change?") before retrying the question verbally or textually.
  • Screen A8 (Side-by-Side Attempt Comparison): Renders a comparative diff highlighting score improvements (e.g., +4.0 in Evidence) and detected behavioral improvements.
  • Screen A9 & Longitudinal Progress: Stores attempt histories in SQLite; renders a 5-parameter time-series graph (Overall, Relevance, Specificity, Evidence, Structure) across attempts with an instant reset button.
  • Screen B3 (Real-Time Voice Simulation Room): Animated Interviewer Avatar (Dr. Marcus Vance) with halo ripples and audio visualizers. Speaks prompts aloud, auto-detects user speech turns, and generates real-time adaptive follow-ups.
  • Screens B6–B8 (Post-Interview Diagnostic Debrief): Comprehensive debrief summarizing performance across questions, with question-by-question breakdown, selective retry, and longitudinal progress tracking.

5. Empirical User Evaluation & Findings

A formal usability study was conducted with N=6 student participants in the HCI Laboratory at IIT Gandhinagar. Participants represented undergraduate and Master's students in Computer Science, Cognitive Science, and HCI preparing for campus placements.

We utilized a within-subjects counterbalanced study design where participants engaged with both Prototype A (instructional cycle) and Prototype B (voice simulation). Usability was quantified using John Brooke's (1996) 10-item System Usability Scale (SUS), automated task completion timing, and semi-structured qualitative think-aloud interviews.

6. Ethics & Risk Assessment

Deploying AI-driven evaluative interfaces requires rigorous protections against evaluative anxiety, algorithmic bias, and biometric privacy risks:

Identified Risk Severity / Impact Implemented Mitigation Protocol
Evaluative Anxiety & Fatigue Moderate Capped simulation sessions to 15–20 minutes; integrated voluntary pause and skip controls; framed feedback as formative coaching rather than summative testing.
Biometric Voice Privacy High All speech recognition executes in the client browser sandbox; zero raw audio recordings or biometric voiceprints are stored or transmitted. All data is anonymized with P01–P06 identifiers.
Voluntary Participation High Digital informed consent verified prior to testing; participants retained the unconditional right to pause or withdraw without academic consequence.
Algorithmic Opacity & Bias Moderate Scores are anchored in deterministic, inspectable rubrics. Sentence-level audit trails provide full transparency into how scores are derived.

7. Conclusion & Future Directions

The CS 435 High-Fidelity Prototype successfully reconciles conversational realism with transparent instructional coaching. By synthesizing real-time voice interaction, inspectable rubric scoring, and metacognitive retry loops, the application offers an accessible, zero-cost practice platform for students. Future work will investigate non-verbal communication tracking via WebRTC video and multi-agent panel interview simulations.
